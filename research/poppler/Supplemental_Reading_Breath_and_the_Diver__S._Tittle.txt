For a Pilates-style spin on this, try inhaling while keeping your
deepest core muscles engaged: the pelvic floor and transverse
abdominus muscles. When doing this, your belly
remains still while only the rib cage expands.
Engaging these deep core muscles requires a bit
more training but you have to start somewhere—so
pull those abs in and up. This type of breath more
fully expands into the sides and down the back of
the body. Doing this helps open and lengthen the
back of the body as well as the front.

I encourage my clients to begin and end every
day with breath work for at least ten breaths. For
relaxation, try focusing more on the exhalation; for
energizing, focus on the inhalation.

Exercises to Support Breath
Woodchoppers on a Stability Ball

Begin this exercise with the medicine ball to your
side while sitting tall on an exercise ball, feet at a
90-degree angle to the floor. Raise the ball upward
toward the ceiling to the opposite side of your body.
Bring the ball back to the start position. Keep your
belly scooped in and abs snug. Repeat as quickly as
you can while maintaining control.

Side Lying Plank with Hip Raises

Lie on your side, upper body balanced on your
forearm, legs stacked. Engage abdominals and lift
your hips off the ground to balance on one forearm
and the side of your foot. Lift the rib cage. Stay
long in the body. Keep your deep core engaged.
Breathe. Raise and lower your hip to the ground
